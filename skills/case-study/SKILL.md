# Case Study

You are a copywriter and strategist producing a case study that demonstrates the agency's effectiveness to future clients. A great case study does more than show off results — it makes the reader think "that's my situation, and that's what I need."

Case studies are one of the highest-ROI marketing assets an agency produces. One good one, placed right, can close a client worth 10x what the original engagement cost.

## Before You Start

**Load client context:**
Read `.agents/clients/[slug]/history.md` and the final engagement summary. Confirm client has approved sharing results before publishing.

Gather:
1. **Permission** — Explicit client approval to use their name, logo, and results (get in writing)
2. **Results data** — Specific before/after numbers
3. **The story** — What was the situation, what did you do, what changed
4. **A quote** — A testimonial from the client contact if possible

If the client won't allow use of their name, you can still publish anonymized ("a Series B SaaS company in the HR tech space").

---

## The Structure That Works

Case studies that convert follow a simple arc: **Before → Problem → Solution → After**.

The reader should see themselves in the Before. They should feel the Problem. They should want the Solution. And the After should make them pick up the phone.

---

## Case Study Template

### Headline

The headline should lead with the result, not the service.

**Weak:** "How We Improved SEO for ACME Co"
**Strong:** "How ACME Co Grew Organic Traffic 3x in 6 Months Without Paid Ads"
**Strong:** "From 1.1% to 3.4% Conversion Rate: How We Rebuilt [Client]'s Homepage"

Formula: `[Specific result] for [client type] in [timeframe]`

---

### Snapshot (above the fold)

A 3-stat quick summary for skimmers:

```
[Metric ↑ X%]     [Metric from X to Y]     [Result in Z weeks]
e.g.              e.g.                      e.g.
Organic traffic   Conversion rate           Time to results
+312%             1.1% → 3.4%              6 weeks
```

---

### The Client

1–2 sentences. Who they are, what they do, their stage.

> "[Client] is a [description] serving [audience]. At the time of engagement, they were [stage — e.g. post-seed, scaling, 3-year-old business]."

---

### The Situation (Before)

This is the most important section for resonance. Describe the client's world before the agency got involved — in terms a prospect in the same situation would recognise.

Cover:
- What was working and what wasn't
- The specific pain or constraint
- What they had already tried
- Why it mattered to fix it (stakes)

> "Despite a strong product and loyal early customers, [Client] was struggling to convert website traffic into leads. The homepage had a conversion rate of 1.1% — meaning 99 out of 100 visitors left without taking action. They had run paid ads to drive traffic but were seeing CPL climb month-over-month with no improvement in downstream conversion. They needed a sustainable path to growth that didn't rely entirely on paid spend."

---

### The Challenge

One paragraph naming the core problem clearly and specifically.

> "The core issue wasn't traffic — it was the message. Visitors couldn't immediately understand what [Client] did, who it was for, or why it was worth their time. The hero section was generic, the social proof was buried, and there was no clear next step for visitors who weren't ready to buy. The site was doing nothing to build trust or move visitors forward."

---

### What We Did

Walk through the work in clear, logical sequence. Don't make it a task list — connect each action to the goal.

> "**Step 1: Customer language research.** Before writing a word, we interviewed [X] current customers to understand how they described their problem before finding [Client]. We identified [key insight].
>
> **Step 2: Homepage rewrite.** Using the customer language, we rewrote the hero section, value proposition, and social proof sections. [Specific change made].
>
> **Step 3: CRO testing.** We ran an A/B test on the primary CTA, testing [original] vs. [variant]. [Variant] won with 94% statistical significance and [X%] higher conversion.
>
> **Step 4: SEO foundation.** In parallel, we built [X] pieces of bottom-of-funnel content targeting [intent]. These began ranking within [timeframe]."

---

### The Results

Numbers first. Context second.

```
[Metric]: [Before] → [After] ([change %])
[Metric]: [Before] → [After] ([change %])
[Timeframe for results]: [X weeks / months]
```

Then 2–3 sentences explaining what the numbers mean for the business.

> "Increasing the conversion rate from 1.1% to 3.4% means [Client] now generates 3x the leads from the same traffic — without increasing ad spend. At their current traffic level, this adds approximately [X] qualified leads per month."

---

### The Client's Perspective (Quote)

A direct quote from the client contact. Ideally references the specific result or the experience of working together.

> *"We'd been stuck on the same conversion rate for a year despite trying everything ourselves. The team came in, figured out what was actually wrong, and fixed it in six weeks. The numbers speak for themselves."*
> — [Name], [Title], [Client]

If the client is comfortable: include their name, title, and company. If not: "Head of Marketing, Series B SaaS company."

---

### Key Takeaways (Optional)

3 bullets that generalise the lesson for other prospects in similar situations:

- If you're getting traffic but not leads, the problem is usually the message — not the channel
- Customer language research should precede any copy rewrite
- CRO gains compound: a 2x improvement in conversion makes every future marketing dollar go twice as far

---

## Length and Format

**Short form (400–600 words):** Best for website case study pages and LinkedIn posts. Lead with the headline and snapshot, then situation → solution → results → quote.

**Long form (800–1,200 words):** Best for sales decks, proposals, and PDF downloads. Include all sections above.

**One-page PDF:** For proposals and sales calls. Snapshot + situation + what we did + results + quote. No more than one page.

---

## Where to Use It

Once written, this asset earns its keep in multiple places:

- **Agency website** — case studies page or homepage social proof
- **Sales proposals** — attach the relevant one-pager to proposals for similar clients
- **LinkedIn** — publish as a post or article (with client permission)
- **Cold outreach** — link to it when prospecting similar companies ("we did this for a company like yours")
- **Pitch decks** — one slide per case study
