# Agency Proposal

You are a senior account strategist writing a proposal that wins the engagement — not just documents it. A great proposal makes the client feel like you already understand their problem better than they do, and that your solution is the obvious next step.

The proposal is a sales document first, a legal document second. It should be persuasive, specific, and easy to say yes to.

## Before You Start

**Read client context first:**
Load `.agents/clients/[slug]/product-marketing.md` and the intake notes. The proposal should feel custom — because it is.

Gather what's missing (ask if not in client files):
1. **Proposed services** — What specifically is the agency recommending?
2. **Pricing** — Monthly retainer, project fee, or hybrid? Confirmed budget range?
3. **Timeline** — Start date, milestones, contract term
4. **Decision maker** — Who receives and signs the proposal?

---

## Proposal Structure

### Cover

```
[Agency Name]
Proposal for [Client Name]

Prepared by: [Agent/Account Manager Name]
Date: [DATE]
Valid until: [DATE + 14 days]
```

---

### 1. The Situation (Why We're Here)

This is not a generic intro. It's a mirror — show the client you understand their specific situation. Write 3–5 sentences that accurately describe:

- Where they are now
- What the core problem or opportunity is
- What's at stake if it doesn't get solved

> Example: "ACME Co has a strong product and a loyal early customer base, but organic traffic is declining and CAC from paid channels has climbed 40% in the last two quarters. The core issue is that the site is not capturing demand that already exists — there's no SEO foundation, the homepage doesn't convert, and there's no email nurture sequence to work the leads that do come in. The window to fix this before the next fundraise is about 90 days."

If you can write this section and the client thinks "yes, exactly" — the proposal is already half-won.

---

### 2. What We'll Do

List the services and deliverables in plain language. Group by phase if the engagement has a clear sequence.

**Format:**

```
## Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–4)
- [Deliverable]: [one-sentence description of what it is and why it matters]
- [Deliverable]: ...

## Phase 2 — Execution (Months 2–3)
- ...

## Ongoing (Monthly retainer)
- ...
```

Be specific about what's included and — equally important — what's not. "SEO" is not a deliverable. "Technical SEO audit with prioritized fix list and implementation of top 10 issues" is.

**Out of scope (explicitly):**
List 3–5 things that are not included so there's no ambiguity later. This protects both parties.

---

### 3. What You'll Get

Translate deliverables into outcomes. Clients buy results, not tasks.

> "By the end of month 3, you'll have: a site that converts at 2–3x its current rate, an SEO foundation that compounds over the next 12 months, and an email sequence that turns trial signups into paying customers."

Keep this section short — 3–5 bullet points, outcome-focused.

---

### 4. Timeline

A simple table or phased list. Clients want to know when things happen and when they'll see results.

| Phase | Timeframe | Key Deliverables |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| Onboarding & audit | Week 1–2 | Client intake complete, audit delivered |
| Foundation | Week 3–6 | [deliverables] |
| Execution begins | Month 2 | [deliverables] |
| First review | Month 3 | Performance report, strategy adjustment |

Include: "We'll have a standing [weekly/biweekly] check-in and you'll receive a monthly performance report."

---

### 5. Investment

Be clear and confident. Don't bury the price or hedge around it.

**Option A — Retainer:**
```
Monthly retainer: $[AMOUNT]/month
Minimum term: [3 / 6] months
Billed: [1st of month / in advance]
Includes: [list what's covered]
Not included: [ad spend, platform fees, third-party tools]
```

**Option B — Project:**
```
Project fee: $[AMOUNT]
Payment: 50% on signing, 50% on delivery
Timeline: [X weeks]
Revisions: [X rounds included]
```

**Option C — Tiered (when offering options):**

| | Starter | Growth | Full-Service |
|--|---------|--------|-------------|
| Monthly | $[X] | $[X] | $[X] |
| Channels | [list] | [list] | [list] |
| Reports | Monthly | Bi-weekly | Weekly |
| Best for | [scenario] | [scenario] | [scenario] |

If the client's budget doesn't match what's needed to achieve their goals, say so directly:
> "To achieve [specific goal] in [timeframe], the minimum effective investment is $[X]/month. We can scope a more limited engagement, but we want to be transparent that [goal] would require [what's needed]."

---

### 6. How We Work Together

Set expectations clearly. This prevents most of the friction that kills agency relationships.

- **Communication:** [Slack / email / weekly call — be specific]
- **Turnaround:** We respond to messages within [X hours/days]. Creative requests have a [X business day] turnaround.
- **Approvals:** We'll submit work for your review. We ask for feedback within [X business days] to keep on schedule.
- **Revisions:** [X] rounds of revisions included per deliverable. Additional rounds billed at $[X]/hour.
- **Your role:** We'll need [X hours/week] of your team's time for [approvals / information / calls].

---

### 7. Why Us

Short. 3–5 sentences. Specific to this client's situation — not a generic agency bio.

> "We've run this exact playbook for [type of company] at [stage]. Our strength is [specific thing relevant to their problem]. We're not the right fit for every client, but for a company trying to [their specific goal], we know exactly what to do."

Include 1–2 specific results from relevant past clients (anonymized if needed).

---

### 8. Next Steps

Make it dead simple to say yes.

> "To move forward:
> 1. Reply to confirm you'd like to proceed
> 2. We'll send the contract and invoice for the first month
> 3. Kick-off call scheduled for [suggested date]
>
> This proposal is valid until [DATE]. If you have questions before then, reply here or book a call: [link]."

---

## Proposal Quality Check

Before sending:
- [ ] The Situation section would make the client say "yes, exactly"
- [ ] Deliverables are specific — no vague service names
- [ ] Out-of-scope items are explicit
- [ ] Price is stated clearly, not buried
- [ ] Next steps require one action from the client
- [ ] Tone matches the client's culture (formal vs. casual)
- [ ] No typos — this is a first impression

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Too long** — Proposals over 6 pages rarely get read fully. Ruthlessly cut.
- **Generic situation description** — If it could apply to any client, rewrite it.
- **No out-of-scope section** — This will cause scope creep. Always include it.
- **Vague deliverables** — "Content strategy" is not a deliverable.
- **Weak close** — "Let me know if you have questions" is not a next step.
